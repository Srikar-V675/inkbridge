# 0078: Stroke kinds classifier

INK-121. Heads, underlines and containers are strokes like any other, so the chunker (INK-030) clusters an arrow head into its own tiny chunk, an underline into the word above it only by luck, and a box around notes into one huge chunk. The v4 design labels every stroke first and clusters second (tech ref v4 section 4, "Classifier").

Decision:
- `inkbridge.canvas.strokes` (pure, next to `sketch.py`, which it reuses for `usable`, `_tip` and `_length`). `analyse(elements)` returns `StrokeKinds(kinds, head_of, scale)`; `classify(elements)` is `analyse(...).kinds`. `kinds` maps element id to `writing`, `container`, `connector`, `head`, `underline` or `scribble`; `head_of` maps a head id to the connector it belongs to; `scale` is T, the writing's median extent (0.0 with no strokes).
- Keys are live (not deleted, not `customData.draft`) usable freedraw strokes and native `arrow`/`line` (always `connector`). Text, rectangles, ellipses, diamonds and frames are not classified and never keys. A dot is `writing`. Malformed elements are skipped, never raised on.
- T is two passes: the median extent of all strokes, then the median of those at most 1.5x that, floored at 4 px. Every size test is a multiple of T, so the rules hold at any zoom. Heads are small strokes and count in T; writing normally outnumbers them (tested with 14 arrows among 40 words).
- Draw order is the fractional `index` (string order equals Excalidraw's), the list position when `index` is missing. "Next in draw order" is the next classified stroke in that order; native text and shapes do not count.
- Check order, first match wins: native arrow/line is a connector; a long closed loop (ends within 0.25 diagonal, path at least 1.8 diagonals) is a `container` only if a smaller stroke's centre lies inside its polygon (ray casting after a bbox prefilter), so a looping cursive word stays writing; a tight zigzag (at least 6 direction reversals, path at least 4 diagonals, at least 1 T long) is a `scribble`; long strokes that are straight (net/length above 0.55) or hooked are shaft candidates, resolved in draw order, and a shaft takes the first unclaimed stroke within 2 positions after it that is under 0.6 T and within 0.75 T of one end as its `head`; then a flat stroke (height under 0.15 width, at least 1 T long) with writing just above it and no head evidence is an `underline`; a remaining shaft is a `connector`; the rest is `writing`. Heads are decided before underlines, otherwise a flat arrow with writing above it reads as an underline.
- Writing above an underline: a stroke at most 1.5 T long, not flat, not a head, or a native `text` element, whose centre x lies within the underline's x range and whose bottom edge is at most 0.75 T above the underline's top or overlaps it by at most 0.5 T. Candidates are bucketed by column (2 T wide) once, so the look-up is not quadratic.
- A hook is a straight shaft (start to tip over path to tip above 0.55) with a trailing barb of at most 0.4 of the path. The plan only asked for the barb limit; the straight-shaft part is added because the last loop of a cursive word pulls back at its end and otherwise reads as a barb (regression test `test_cursive_word_is_not_scribble`).
- The plan listed an `UNDERLINE_COVER` constant next to "centre x lies within the underline's x range"; the range test alone is implemented and the constant dropped.
- Thresholds are module constants, all start values. E5 on 12 to 15 real pages tunes them. Not built here: wiring into the chunker or `ChunkerConfig` (INK-122), hashes (unchanged), DB, API. 824 elements classify in about 8 ms.

Known limits: a head drawn before its shaft, or more than 2 strokes after it, stays `writing`; a flat arrow with no head under writing reads as an underline; a small stroke written right after an underline, near its end, is taken as a head and turns the underline into a connector; a page of single-stroke cursive paragraphs makes T large; with fewer than about 5 strokes T comes from those strokes themselves; a closed container with no ink inside it, such as an empty box, is `writing` (an empty box has no chunk to organise).
